How this works

This page surfaces statistical trends from EPL match history and per-player data - where a team or player's recent form sits relative to a standard bookmaker line. It's not betting advice, and the odds shown are a modeled approximation, not a live quote.

Reliability tiers

Every signal is tagged by how often the call has actually come in, blended across both sides of the matchup:

Strong - 75%+ Moderate - 60-74% Weak - under 60%

Fixtures tab

  • Each card is one upcoming match, with a signal row per market (goals, corners, shots on target, cards) for each team.
  • The bar shows the edge: how far a team's own rate sits from the league average for that market.
  • On Strong and Moderate rows, the dropdown lets you try a different line - hit rate and modeled odds update immediately, using the same alt-line ladder the server already computed, so the numbers never drift from the real calculation.
  • Strong rows that clear a 5% edge over their historical odds floor show a "Take at X+ odds" chip, grounded in how bets predicted at roughly that hit rate have actually performed across thousands of historical bets since 2019-20 - not just this one sample.
  • Click a team's name to open its full match-by-match history.

All Signals tab

Every team signal across every fixture, flattened into one sortable, filterable list - useful for scanning the whole round for Strong calls at once instead of card by card.

Players tab

  • Covers goals, shots, shots on target, and assists - the markets with real per-match history available (Understat). Cards aren't included; the source only has season totals for those.
  • Over-only: under calls on these markets are almost always trivially true for low-usage players (a defender "under 0.5 goals" hits ~100% but prices near 1.01) and were cluttering the list with zero-value calls, so they're filtered out entirely.
  • Filter by game, tier, or market along the top; click the Hit rate or Modeled odds column header to sort by it, click again to flip direction.
  • Strong and Moderate rows get the same line-picker dropdown as the team markets - pick a different line and the row's hit rate, odds, and tier update in place.
  • Which players are tracked per team is a best guess at who's likely to feature (most appearances last season), not a confirmed lineup.

Bet slip

  • Click the small "+" next to any line (Fixtures tab or Players tab) to add it as a leg - it adds exactly the line currently showing, so pick your line first, then add it.
  • Open the slip via the "Slip" button in the bottom-right corner. Each leg's line can still be changed from inside the slip, without going back to find the row it came from.
  • "Minimum odds to take" is the headline number: for legs sharing a fixture, it uses correlation-adjusted (true SGM) odds - see below; for legs on different fixtures, straightforward multiplication is already correct, since those are genuinely independent events. "...if every leg were independent" is shown alongside it so you can see how much the correlation adjustment actually moved the number.
  • "Total implied edge" is a separate figure: the compounded 5% edge from however many legs are backed by the historical odds floor - (1.05^n - 1). Legs using modeled odds only (no historical floor - every player leg, plus any team leg below Strong tier) don't add to this, since modeled odds carry no built-in edge cushion of their own.
  • Fill in the odds you were actually quoted, your stake, and which platform, then hit "Place bet" - it saves the whole slip as one tracked bet (multi or single) and clears the slip. See the Tracker tab below for what happens next.

True SGM odds

Naive multiplication assumes every leg is independent - true across different fixtures, false for legs from the SAME match, where a shared factor (how open or cagey the game turned out, its overall pace) pushes markets to move together. This is measured directly, not assumed: every pair of team markets has a "lift" factor computed from real match history (how often they actually landed together vs what independence would predict), and the slip applies it whenever 2+ legs share a fixture. A lift above 1.0 means the two events tend to happen together more than chance; below 1.0 means they pull against each other. This is a genuine, data-grounded adjustment, not a full joint model - it's computed at each market's standard anchor line and applied approximately to whatever line you've actually picked, and it isn't modeled at all for player-leg correlations yet (a player leg still contributes its own probability, just not correlated against the rest of the group). Shown in the slip whenever it applies, alongside the naive figure for comparison.

Tracker tab

  • Every bet placed from the slip shows up here automatically, with its legs, platform, odds, stake, and potential return.
  • Once a leg's fixture has been played and results are in the page's data, it grades itself Won or Lost on page load - no action needed. A multi/SGM needs every leg to win; one confirmed loss settles the whole bet as Lost even if other legs haven't played yet, and it only settles Won once every leg has.
  • The P/L bar at the top shows running profit/loss for the last 7 days and all-time, from settled bets only (pending bets don't count yet).
  • Remove a bet with the "×" on its row if it was tracked by mistake - this can't be undone.
  • The Analytics sub-tab charts cumulative profit over time across settled bets, plus win rate, total staked, and ROI.

Team Trends

Every tracked team's own rolling average (10-38 games, see methodology), across all markets. This is the raw material the matchup projections above are built from.

Methodology. Each team's own game sample is adaptive: early in a season it's a 10-game window bridging in the tail of last season (dropping its oldest game as each new current-season game arrives), and once a team has played 10 games in the current season alone, the bridge stops permanently and every new game just accumulates for the rest of that season (up to 38) rather than pushing anything out - so trends lean on last season's form early on and become pure current-season form as the season matures. Click any fixture's team names to see that exact game-by-game history. Each team gets a strength rating per market: its own rolling rate divided by the current league average (1.00 = exactly average, 1.30 = 30% above, 0.70 = 30% below). Matchup projections multiply attack strength × defense strength × league average - the standard expected-goals-style approach used across football analytics - so a big raw number racked up against weak opposition isn't treated the same as the same raw number against strong opposition. Lines are anchored at the standard main line real bookmakers actually use for each market (1.5 goals, 5.5 corners, 4.5 shots on target, 3.5 total cards) rather than derived from the projection - most books keep these standardized across teams and let the price reflect team strength instead of moving the line, so a heavy-corner side still gets offered 5.5, just at a much shorter price. Pick a different line from the dropdown on any signal to see an alt-line ladder around that anchor. Each line also shows a modeled fair odds estimate (Poisson model of the projection, no bookmaker margin) - an approximation to sanity-check against real quoted odds, not a substitute for them, since no live odds feed covers these specific team-level alt-line markets for free. The headline number is the projection versus the market's league average, so +2.1 means the projection sits 2.1 above what's typical for that market. The two hit-rate chips under each signal are each team's OWN historical rate AT the current line, on their OWN side of the market (e.g. how often the scorer's own games cleared it, and separately how often the conceder's own games did too) - always labeled by name, never blended into one number. Match cards sums both teams' own card rates directly (cards are additive, not a for/against pair) and checks each team's own match history for how often their combined-with-opponent total cleared the line - note real books usually price cards as "booking points" (yellow=10, red=25) rather than a raw count, so this market's line is a rougher match to a real cards market than the others. Newly promoted teams with fewer than 6 top-flight games are topped up with their most recent Championship form, flagged inline. Data: football-data.co.uk.
